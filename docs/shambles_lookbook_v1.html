SHAMBLES Lookbook — Six Design Directions

🌐 marcdeller.com ✉️ marc@marcdeller.com

Same component, six personalities

Every preview below renders the identical live matchup card — two managers, two scores, one thing to watch — so you are comparing the design language, not the content. All six assume the same Flask backend and the same five tabs; the theme is a stylesheet swap, not a rebuild.

The brief was flashy, fun and simple. That rules out anything with a settings panel, and it rules out the flat card grid your science apps use. SHAMBLES should be unmistakably not CODSWALLOP.

DIRECTION 01

BROADCAST BOOTH

Sunday night football graphics package. Lower thirds, condensed numerals, an amber lead indicator.

Palette

Type

Bebas Neue for names and scores, Inter for body, Roboto Mono for probabilities. Condensed caps are what make it read as broadcast rather than dashboard.

Signature move

Score changes animate as a slot-machine roll, and the lower third slides up from the bottom edge on a scoring play. Motion carries the whole identity.

Best for

Big BoardSwingometer
Risk: the most conventional option here. Done badly it is just a dark dashboard, and the ESPN app is already dark.

THE CHALKBOARD

Palette

Type

Caveat throughout, with a mono fallback for anything that must stay legible at a glance. Slight per-element rotation stops it looking like a font and starts it looking like handwriting.

Signature move

New entries are drawn on with an SVG stroke animation, and settled fines get scrubbed out with a chalk-smudge wipe rather than deleted.

Risk: handwriting fonts and dense numbers fight each other. Fine for verdicts and awards, poor for a table of twelve rows.

Type

JetBrains Mono only. Tabular figures mean columns align without a table, which is why this direction fits so much on a phone screen.

Palette

Type

Press Start 2P for anything short and shouty; a clean sans for anything longer than five words, because the pixel face becomes unreadable fast.

Signature move

Scoring plays trigger a full-screen flash, a horn, and a high-score-table entry with the manager's initials. This is the only direction where sound is part of the design rather than a bolt-on.

Best for

Big BoardFull-screen mode
Risk: exhausting for a four-hour session, and the pixel font caps your information density at about six words per line. Consider it a mode, not the whole app.

THE BACK PAGE

Tabloid sports section. Screaming headline, red masthead, newsprint cream, zero mercy.

Palette

Type

Anton for headlines, Archivo Black for the masthead, Inter for standfirst and body. The headline scales with the size of the story — a 40-point blowout gets 40-point type.

Signature move

The app writes prose, not panels. Every week auto-generates a back page from templated narrative rules fired by the underlying stats. It is the only direction that makes The Receipts feel inevitable rather than bolted on.

Best for

The ReceiptsWeekly recapShareable
Risk: it is a reading layout, so it suits Monday morning better than live Sunday. Pair it with a livelier theme for in-play tabs.

Palette

Type

Anton for names and scores, Roboto Mono for the stat block. Holographic sheen is a single rotated gradient pseudo-element — cheap, and it sells the whole conceit.

Signature move

Cards tilt to the cursor or the phone's gyroscope and the foil moves with them. Rarity tiers earned by performance: a 150-point week mints a gold card that stays in the album all season.

Best for

StandingsManager profilesMobile
Risk: cards are wonderful for two managers and unwieldy for twelve. Needs a compact list view underneath, which is a second layout to maintain.

Making the theme a swap, not a decision

You do not have to pick one today. Build the templates once against semantic classes, keep every colour and font in a per-theme variables file, and the choice becomes a two-line change in base.html.

shambles/
├── app.py                 # blueprint per tab
├── espn/
│   ├── client.py          # holds espn_s2 + SWID, one place only
│   └── cache.py           # 30 s TTL, shared across all viewers
├── static/themes/
│   ├── broadcast.css      # each defines the SAME variable names
│   ├── chalkboard.css
│   └── backpage.css
└── templates/
    ├── base.html          # <link href="themes/{{ theme }}.css">
    └── partials/
        └── matchup.html   # rendered standalone for live swaps
  • Server-side rendering, htmx for liveness. A hx-get="/partials/matchup/3" hx-trigger="every 30s" attribute gives you live updates with no front-end framework and no JSON serialisation layer. Jinja renders the same partial for the first paint and every refresh after.
  • Server-Sent Events for the loud moments. Polling handles scores; a single /stream SSE endpoint pushes touchdowns instantly so the horn fires when the room sees the play, not 25 seconds later. SSE is one-directional and needs no websocket stack.
  • One cache, all viewers. Twelve phones in a bar must not mean twelve times the ESPN traffic. Cache in the Flask process and serve every client from it — ESPN sees one poll regardless of how many people are watching.
  • Tabs as routes, not JavaScript. /big-board, /cheer, /swing, /receipts, /multiverse. Deep-linkable, back-button correct, and the bar TV can be parked on one URL all afternoon.
  • Full-screen mode is a query param. ?tv=1 drops the nav, doubles every font size, and disables interaction. Same template, one conditional.
